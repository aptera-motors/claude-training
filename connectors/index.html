Connectors & Skills

Claude isn't just a chatbot. At Aptera it's wired into your actual tools — and it knows some tricks.

⚡ In 30 seconds

A connector is a secure bridge that lets Claude read and write a tool on your behalf. A skill is a packaged, repeatable workflow you can run by name. Together they turn Claude from a clever assistant into something that actually does work inside your systems.

Out of the box, Claude knows a lot — but it doesn't know what's in your Jira board, your Confluence wiki, or last month's Ramp statement. Connectors fix that. They give Claude permission to look things up, create records, and take actions in the tools you already use. You stay in control: Claude acts as you, with your permissions, and asks before it does anything consequential.

Connected today

These are the tools Claude is already wired into at Aptera. Open Co-Work and start asking.

🎫 Jira

Read, create, and update issues. Claude can search across projects, pull sprint status, or log a new bug without you touching the Jira UI.

Try asking:

"What are the open issues in project OI assigned to me right now?"

📚 Confluence

Read, create, and update pages. Ask Claude to find documentation, summarize a space, or draft a new page and publish it — straight from a conversation.

Try asking:

"Find the onboarding docs in Confluence and give me a bullet summary of what's there."

💳 Ramp

Transactions, bills, reimbursements, and spend reporting. Ask Claude to pull your recent charges, flag anything that needs a receipt, or summarize team spend by category.

Try asking:

"Show me my transactions from the last 30 days and flag any that are missing receipts."

📨 Microsoft 365

Outlook mail and calendar, SharePoint file search, and Teams chat history. Claude can check your schedule, hunt down a file, or search chat for that decision from last month.

Try asking:

"Search my Teams messages for any mention of the solar hood pilot from the last two weeks."

⚠️ Connectors act as you

When Claude uses a connector, it's acting with your identity and your permissions. If you ask Claude to create a Jira issue, it creates it as you. If you ask it to send an email, it sends it from your address. Claude will always confirm before taking actions that write or modify data — but you're the one responsible for what gets done. Treat it like handing your keyboard to a very capable colleague: you're still accountable.

Skills

A skill is a packaged, repeatable workflow you can invoke by name — like calling a function instead of writing it from scratch every time. Skills combine multiple steps (including connector calls) into a single, reliable flow. The big one available right now:

📝 Meeting Minutes skill Co-Work

Drop a Teams meeting transcript (VTT or TXT file) into Co-Work, then run the Meeting Minutes skill. It takes care of the rest — formatted Confluence notes and Jira action items, all linked together. Here's exactly how it goes:

  1. 1 Upload the transcript. Drag your .vtt or .txt Teams export into the Co-Work file area. Claude reads the whole thing.
  2. 2 Claude confirms the details. It identifies attendees and the meeting title, then asks you to verify before writing anything. You approve, correct, or add context.
  3. 3 Confluence page is created. A formatted meeting minutes page goes into the correct Confluence space — attendees, summary, discussion points, decisions, and action items.
  4. 4 Jira issues are filed. Each action item becomes a Jira ticket, assigned to the right person, linked back to the Confluence page. No copy-paste, no missed items.
  5. 5 You review and share. Claude gives you links to everything it created. You spot-check, tweak anything that needs it, and send the Confluence link to the team.

A 90-minute meeting that used to take 45 minutes to document now takes about 3.

🧪 Try a connector

Open Co-Work and use the Confluence connector to run a real search. Paste this prompt exactly:

Search Confluence for our onboarding docs and summarize what's there. Tell me: what topics are covered, what looks out of date, and what seems to be missing for a new employee.

Claude will query Confluence on your behalf, pull back the relevant pages, and give you a structured summary — no Confluence tab required.

🔑 Key takeaways

  • Connectors give Claude live access to your tools — Jira, Confluence, Ramp, and Microsoft 365 are wired in today.
  • Skills are named, repeatable workflows — the Meeting Minutes skill turns a Teams transcript into Confluence notes + Jira tickets in minutes.
  • Connectors act as you and with your permissions — you're always the one approving consequential actions.
