Manual Looping

Most of us treat AI like a search engine — type something in, take the first answer, move on. The unlock is treating Claude as a collaborator you iterate with, not a vending machine you query once.

⚡ In 30 seconds

One prompt gets you a draft. Looping gets you something worth using. Send a prompt, read what comes back, then refine — adjust the tone, push for depth, reformat, try a new angle — until it fits the work. You don't need to be a great prompt writer. You just need to be a good reviewer.

What is manual looping?

Manual looping just means following up. You send a prompt, get a response, and then refine — adjust the tone, push for more depth, reformat the output, try a different angle. You stay in the conversation and build toward something genuinely useful, rather than accepting the first draft or starting over from scratch.

Think of it like working with a talented contractor. You don't hand them a napkin sketch, walk away, and hope for a finished product. You review, give feedback, and build toward something right together.

A real-world example

Here's a strong opening prompt — one that gives Claude enough context to produce something useful on the first pass:

I need to draft an email to a new parts supplier we're onboarding — a small machine shop called Precision Fabrication Group based in San Diego. This is our first communication after they signed our NDA. The goal is to kick off the supplier qualification process. They'll need to submit three things: a completed W-9, a copy of their quality certification (we accept ISO 9001 or AS9100), and a filled-out Aptera Supplier Questionnaire (I'll attach the form separately). Tone should be professional but warm — we want them to feel like a partner, not like they're being processed. Keep it under 200 words.

That's a prompt worth sending. It names the supplier, states the context, lists the exact deliverables, specifies tone, and sets a length constraint. Claude now has everything it needs to produce a first draft that's genuinely close.

Then the loop begins:

Good. Now tighten the opening line — it's a little stiff. And move the list of required documents into a numbered list so it's easier to scan.

Perfect. One more pass — swap 'please submit the following' for something that sounds less like a form letter.

Three turns. Specific feedback each time. The result is something you'd actually send.

Why it beats the "perfect prompt" approach

There's a temptation to front-load everything into one massive, hyper-detailed prompt. Resist it. Iteration is faster and more reliable — perfect prompts take longer to write and still rarely produce perfect outputs.

Manual looping lets you:

  • Start strong, not perfect — a good context-rich prompt gets you 80% there on the first pass.
  • Course-correct early — before you're deep in the wrong direction.
  • Stay in control — you shape the output through dialogue rather than trying to anticipate everything upfront.

The best outputs usually come after a few refinements — and the moves are simpler than most people think: "Make it shorter." "Make it more casual." "Give me another version of option two." "Change this section but keep the rest."

One thing to watch

Looping works best when you're refining in the right direction. If an early response is fundamentally off — wrong scope, wrong format, wrong assumptions — you have two good options:

✏️ Option 1 · Edit the original prompt

Claude lets you go back and edit a previous message. Often the cleanest fix — you correct the source of the problem rather than layering corrections on a bad foundation. Use this when the issue is how you set things up, not just the output.

🆕 Option 2 · Start a new conversation

If the whole direction needs to change, a fresh start with a better prompt beats steering a derailed thread. Not wasted effort — what you learned in the first conversation is exactly what makes the second one better.

That said, starting fresh doesn't mean losing everything. Claude can remember context from past conversations, and you can resume a previous chat right where you left off. The richest context is always within the active session — so for complex, multi-step work, staying in one conversation gives you the most continuity.

🪟 A note on the context window

As you loop, Claude holds the whole exchange in its context window — its working memory for the session. In very long sessions that memory fills up and recall of earlier turns can fade. It's worth knowing when to summarize, save your work, and start fresh — which is exactly what the Context Window lesson covers next.

🤖 Ready for the next level?

Manual looping is you having a back-and-forth with Claude. The natural next step is agentic looping — stepping back and letting Claude run its own loop until the work is done. See Agentic Looping.

🔑 Key takeaways

  • One prompt is a draft. Looping is how you get something worth using.
  • Start strong, see what comes back, then refine with short, specific feedback.
  • If a response is fundamentally off, edit the original prompt or start fresh — don't pile fixes on a bad foundation.
  • You don't need to be a great prompt writer — just a good reviewer.
