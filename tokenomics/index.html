Tokenomics

Great results without burning money. Here's what you actually need to know.

⚡ In 30 seconds

Claude charges per token (roughly ¾ of a word). Every message in and out costs tokens. Pick the right-sized model for the job, keep conversations focused, and you'll get better answers for less money. Win-win.

What even is a token?

Think of tokens as chunks of text — roughly ¾ of a word each. "Aerodynamics" is one token. "Hey Claude, can you help me with this?" is about ten. Claude reads your whole message (tokens in) and writes a reply (tokens out). Both sides of that exchange get tallied.

This is not a reason to avoid using Claude. It's a reason to use it smartly. A focused 50-token ask that gets the right answer on the first try beats a rambling 500-token saga that needs three rounds of back-and-forth.

Pick the right model

Anthropic makes four models of Claude. Same brain, different horsepower — and different price tags. Reach for the right one and you'll finish faster and spend less.

⚡

Haiku

Fast & cheap

Reach for it when… you need a quick rewrite, a short answer, a yes/no check, or you're doing something repetitive dozens of times. Haiku is the espresso shot — small, fast, gets the job done.

🚗

Sonnet

The daily driver · best balance

Reach for it when… you're doing real work — summarizing a meeting, drafting an email, analyzing a doc, building something in Co-Work mode. 90% of your Claude time belongs here.

🏋️

Opus

The heavyweight

Reach for it when… the problem is genuinely hard: deep financial analysis, complex multi-step reasoning, evaluating tradeoffs with lots of context. Bring out the big gun when the job demands it.

🧙

Fable

The frontier · premium

Reach for it when… the job is huge and multi-step — especially when you want one model to orchestrate a team of cheaper ones. Powerful and pricey; overkill for everyday tasks. More in Choosing Your Model.

Habits that save money (and get better answers)

The biggest lever isn't model choice — it's how you structure your conversations. A few small habits compound into dramatically better results.

  • Start a fresh chat when you change topics. Every message in a conversation gets re-read from scratch on every turn. A 40-message thread about solar panel specs is expensive context to drag into a totally different HR question.
  • Be specific up front. "Write a three-sentence summary of this status update for the exec team" costs one round-trip. "Summarize this" usually costs three — one attempt plus two rounds of "make it shorter / more formal / less jargon."
  • Don't paste the whole PDF. You don't need to. Paste the one section that's relevant. Claude reads everything you give it every single time.
  • Batch related questions. If you have five questions about the same document, ask all five at once. Five separate chats = five times the overhead.

Context windows, simply

Every turn re-reads the whole conversation from the top, so long, unfocused chats cost more and can start to drift. Want the full picture — how the window fills and when to compact? See the Context Window lesson.

🧪 See the difference yourself

Paste the focused prompt into Claude, note the answer. Then paste the rambling one. Which got there faster?

The focused ask:

Rewrite this sentence to be more confident and under 20 words: 'We are hoping to potentially explore the possibility of launching a pilot program sometime in Q3 if conditions allow.'

The rambling version (costs more, takes longer):

Hey so I have this sentence in a doc and I'm not totally sure if it's the right vibe, like it feels a bit wishy-washy? Can you maybe look at it and tell me what you think and potentially rewrite it? Here it is: 'We are hoping to potentially explore the possibility of launching a pilot program sometime in Q3 if conditions allow.' I think it needs to sound more confident but I'm not sure, what do you think is the best approach here?

🔑 Key takeaways

  • Tokens are the currency — every word in and out counts. Use Claude smartly, not sparingly.
  • Match the model to the job: Haiku for quick tasks, Sonnet for daily work, Opus for heavy analysis, Fable for the biggest multi-step projects.
  • Fresh chats are cheaper and better — don't drag stale context into a new topic.
