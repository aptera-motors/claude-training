Safety & Data

Claude is a powerful coworker — not a magic oracle. Two rules cover 90% of it: be careful what you share, and verify what you ship.

⚡ In 30 seconds

Don't paste passwords, customer PII, or NDA-covered material into Claude. Do verify numbers, dates, and legal/financial claims before sending them anywhere. On enterprise accounts, your conversations don't train the model. When in doubt, ask Jake.

Claude is a coworker, not a crystal ball

Think of Claude like a brilliant new hire who reads fast, writes well, and never sleeps — but who also occasionally makes things up with complete confidence. (We call that hallucination.) That's not a dealbreaker; it just means you treat Claude's output the same way you'd treat a first draft from any colleague: useful, but worth a second look before it goes anywhere official.

Two rules cover the vast majority of situations. Master these and you're good:

  1. Be careful what you share. Claude can only work with what you give it — so give it the right stuff.
  2. Verify what you ship. You own anything you send out. Claude helps you draft; you're responsible for what's in it.

Never paste these ❌

Some things just don't belong in a chat window with any AI tool:

  • ❌Passwords, API keys, or credentials — of any kind, for any system.
  • ❌Customer PII in bulk — names, emails, addresses, purchase records. One name in a one-off question is different from a CSV dump.
  • ❌NDA-covered material you wouldn't forward externally — if you'd hesitate to email it to a vendor, don't paste it into Claude.
  • ❌Unreleased financial data or board materials — earnings, forecasts, M&A details.

These are totally fine ✓

Don't overthink it. All of this is fair game:

  • ✓Internal docs and presentations — specs, slide decks, process guides.
  • ✓Your own drafts — emails, reports, proposals you're working on.
  • ✓Publicly available information — product pages, press releases, industry articles.
  • ✓Anonymized data — "we had 47 support tickets this week" is fine; a spreadsheet with customer emails is not.
  • ✓Code and configs — as long as they don't contain embedded secrets.

Verify before you ship

Claude can be confidently wrong. It might cite a made-up statistic, get a date wrong by a year, or invent a part number that doesn't exist — all while sounding completely authoritative. This is hallucination, and it's a known quirk of how large language models work.

The fix is simple: double-check the things that matter.

  • Numbers and percentages — always sanity-check against your source data.
  • Dates and deadlines — confirm against the actual calendar or doc.
  • Part numbers and specs — verify in the system of record before passing on.
  • Legal and financial claims — run these past the relevant team before they leave Aptera.

You own what you send. If Claude helped you write it, that's great — but you're the one who signed off. Make it count.

Your data & the model

Short version: your conversations at work do not train Claude.

Aptera uses enterprise/work accounts. Anthropic's enterprise terms explicitly exclude conversation data from model training. What you type into Claude at work stays out of the training pipeline. You're not inadvertently teaching the model your trade secrets.

If you use a personal Claude account on your own time, that's governed by personal account terms — a different story. Stick to the work account for work stuff and you're covered.

When in doubt, ask Jake

Genuinely unsure whether something is safe to share? Email jake@aptera.us. There are no dumb questions here — only unlogged incidents. A quick "hey, is this okay?" takes 30 seconds. Cleaning up an overshare takes considerably longer.

⚡ Extra care with Claude Code

Claude Code doesn't just chat — it executes real commands on real systems. It can create files, delete things, run scripts, and push code. Before you let it proceed with any operation that touches your filesystem or a remote service, read the plan it proposes. Pay extra attention to anything destructive: deleting files, overwriting configs, resetting databases. When in doubt, ask it to explain what a command does before running it.

🔑 Key takeaways

  • Never paste passwords, bulk PII, or NDA-covered material into Claude.
  • Internal docs, your own drafts, public info, and anonymized data are all fine.
  • Claude can hallucinate — always verify numbers, dates, part numbers, and legal/financial claims before they go anywhere official.
  • On enterprise accounts, your conversations are not used to train the model.
  • Claude Code runs real commands — review its plan before letting it execute anything destructive.
  • Unsure? Email jake@aptera.us. No dumb questions.
